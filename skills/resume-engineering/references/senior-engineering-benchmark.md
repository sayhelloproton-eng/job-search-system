# Senior engineering resume benchmark

Research refresh: 2026-08-23.

This file defines the quality floor for senior software-engineering resumes. Truthfulness is necessary but not sufficient: a truthful resume that compresses ten years of engineering into generic one-line duties still fails.

## What strong senior resumes consistently do

A strong senior resume makes the reader understand, quickly:

1. What class of engineering problems this person can own.
2. Which systems/projects prove it.
3. What was hard or risky.
4. What the candidate personally decided or changed.
5. How the mechanism worked at a useful technical level.
6. What changed after the work: reliability, delivery, maintainability, reuse, scale, risk, product capability, or team leverage.

The recent role must not read like a job description. It should read like a portfolio of engineering decisions and outcomes.

## Density floor for senior ICs

For roughly 8+ years of experience, do not optimize for an arbitrary visual length before content quality exists.

- Current template target is a complete Markdown resume, not a page count.
- Most recent/relevant role: normally 4–6 substantive accomplishment bullets or equivalent project blocks.
- Second relevant role: normally 2–4 substantive bullets.
- Oldest role: 1–2 bullets or an equivalent compact paragraph is acceptable.
- Selected flagship projects: normally 1–3 projects, with enough depth to expose context, decisions, mechanism and consequences.

Sparse variants are not the default quality standard. A senior resume must preserve both flagship depth and career continuity.

## Flagship project anatomy

Before writing a flagship project, recover these fields privately:

- one-line context/value: what system or product was this and why did it matter;
- role/scope: what the candidate personally owned;
- hard problem: architecture, migration, scale, reliability, compatibility, developer workflow, or product constraint;
- decision/trade-off: what approach was chosen and why;
- mechanism: enough implementation detail to prove depth;
- outcome: a defensible metric when available, otherwise a concrete engineering consequence;
- interview hook: what technical discussion this bullet can open.

The public resume may compress these fields across 2–4 bullets, but it must not reduce the entire project to a technology inventory.

## Accomplishment bullet quality

A strong bullet normally contains at least three useful signals from this set:

`scope/problem + ownership/decision + mechanism + consequence/result + verified scale`

One signal is a task log. Two signals are often mid-level. Three or more usually create a senior-level interview hook.

Metrics are not mandatory. When no safe number exists, consequences such as safer rollout, controlled migration, reduced duplication, reusable platform capability, recovery behavior, compatibility coverage, or clearer ownership are valid outcomes when evidenced.

## Work experience vs selected projects

Do not duplicate the same sentence in both sections.

## Preserve breadth without flattening depth

A targeted senior resume may choose only 2–4 flagship projects for deep treatment, but it must not erase the rest of a long engineering career.

After flagship projects, keep an `Other representative deliveries` / `其他代表性交付` section when the candidate has meaningful breadth. Use concise one-line entries for real but lower-priority projects so reviewers can see domain range without stealing space from flagship stories.

This section is especially important when historical evidence spans multiple products, platforms, migrations, infrastructure/tooling efforts, or delivery environments. Targeting controls emphasis, not historical deletion.

Human scoring can explicitly reward completeness: if a resume feels truthful but incomplete because relevant ordinary projects disappeared, preserve that feedback and expand breadth in the next revision rather than adding invented impact language.
